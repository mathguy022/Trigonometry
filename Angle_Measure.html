  • Basic algebraic manipulation (solving for variables).

Warm-Up / Review

Question: A full circle represents a complete rotation. How many degrees are in a full circle?

Answer: 360 degrees.

Question: If you cut a pizza into 4 equal slices, what fraction of the pizza is one slice?

Answer: 1/4. In terms of angles, this is 1/4 of 360°, which is 90°.

1. Defining Angles: Degrees vs. Radians

In college mathematics, we primarily use two units to measure angles:

A. Degrees (°)

A degree is defined by dividing a circle into 360 equal parts. This is an arbitrary system based on ancient Babylonian astronomy.

B. Radians (rad)

A radian is a "natural" unit of measurement based on the geometry of the circle itself.

Definition: One radian is the angle subtended at the center of a circle by an arc that is equal in length to the radius of the circle.

Why use Radians? They simplify calculus formulas later on. For example, the derivative of sin(x) is cos(x) only if x is in radians.

The Conversion Factor

Since a full circle is 360° and also 2π radians:

360° = 2π radians

Dividing both sides by 2, we get the fundamental relationship:

180° = π radians

To convert, we use the ratio:

  • Degrees to Radians: Multiply by π180
  • Radians to Degrees: Multiply by 180π

3. Converting Measures

Let's practice converting. Remember: To go from Degrees to Radians, multiply by π180.

Example 1: Convert 135° to radians.

Step 1: Set up the multiplication. 135 · π180
Step 2: Simplify the fraction. Both numbers are divisible by 45. 135 ÷ 45 = 3
180 ÷ 45 = 4
Answer: 3π4 radians.
Example 2: Convert 5π6 radians to degrees.

Step 1: Multiply by 180π. 5π6 · 180π
Step 2: Cancel π and divide 180 by 6. 5 · 30 = 150
Answer: 150°.

4. Standard Position & Coterminal Angles

Standard Position

An angle is in standard position if:

  1. Its vertex is at the origin (0,0).
  2. Its initial side lies along the positive x-axis.

Coterminal Angles

Coterminal angles share the same terminal side. You find them by adding or subtracting full rotations (360° or 2π).

Coterminal Angle = θ + 360° · k (or θ + 2π · k)

Where k is any integer (1, -1, 2, etc.).

Example: Find a positive and a negative coterminal angle for 100°.
Positive: 100° + 360° = 460°
Negative: 100° - 360° = -260°

5. Arc Length and Sector Area

These formulas rely on the proportion of the angle to the whole circle. Note: The angle θ must be in Radians for these standard formulas.

Concept Formula Description
Arc Length (s) s = rθ Length of the curved part of the circle.
Sector Area (A) A = 12r²θ Area of the "pie slice" region.

6. Supplementary and Complementary Angles

These terms describe pairs of angles that add up to specific totals.

  • Complementary Angles: Two angles whose sum is 90° (a right angle).
    Example: 30° and 60°.
  • Supplementary Angles: Two angles whose sum is 180° (a straight line).
    Example: 110° and 70°.

7. Real-World Application

Problem: A car tire has a diameter of 27 inches. If the car travels 1 mile (5280 feet), how many revolutions does the tire make?

Solution Steps:
  1. Find Radius: r = 27 / 2 = 13.5 inches.
  2. Find Circumference (Distance per rev): C = 2πr = 2 · π · 13.5 ≈ 84.82 inches.
  3. Convert Total Distance to Inches: 1 mile = 5280 feet. 5280 · 12 = 63,360 inches.
  4. Divide Total Distance by Circumference: 63,360 / 84.82 ≈ 746.99.
Answer: Approximately 747 revolutions.
